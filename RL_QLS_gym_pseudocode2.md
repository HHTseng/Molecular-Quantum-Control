# RL-QLS: Gymnasium Environment and DQN/qMDP Pseudocode

## 1. Scope

This document gives implementation-oriented pseudocode for the reinforcement-learning quantum-logic spectroscopy (RL-QLS) method in:

- A. Pipi, X. Tao, A. Wu, P. Narang, and D. R. Leibrandt, *Molecular Quantum Control Algorithm Design by Reinforcement Learning*, arXiv:2410.11839v5 / Phys. Rev. Research 8, 033103 (2026).
- The paper's Supplemental Material, especially Secs. SA-SD.

The pseudocode separates three layers:

1. **Physics preprocessing:** build pulse- and measurement-conditioned population maps from time-dependent quantum dynamics.
2. **Interactive Gymnasium environment:** sample one projective-measurement branch per action.
3. **RL training:** train either ordinary sampled Double DQN or the paper's expected-branch quantum-MDP (qMDP) variant.

The qMDP algorithm needs more information than the standard Gymnasium `step()` return: it needs every possible measurement branch and its probability. The environment therefore exposes a deterministic `branch_model(state, action)` method and also returns those branches in `info`.

---

## 2. Mathematical data model

### 2.1 Molecular population state

Let

$$
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{mol}} = \operatorname{span}\{|i\rangle : i=1,\ldots,N_S\},
$$

where $|i\rangle$ is a molecular eigenstate. Between pulse-measurement steps, the paper assumes that motional cooling destroys the relevant coherences. The RL observation is therefore the population vector

$$
s = (s_1,\ldots,s_{N_S})^T \in \Delta_{N_S-1},
$$

where

$$
\Delta_{N_S-1}
= \left\{s\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{N_S}: \mathbf 1^T s = 1\right\}.
$$

The initial state is normally a Boltzmann distribution,

$$
s_{0,i} = \frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{\sum_j e^{-\beta E_j}}.
$$

### 2.2 Actions

The action set is a finite pulse library

$$
\mathcal A = \{0,1,\ldots,N_A-1\}.
$$

An action $a\in\mathcal A$ selects one predesigned molecular Raman sideband pulse, including its frequency, polarization, amplitude/Rabi rate, and duration. The measurement itself is not an action in the implementation described by the paper; a projective motional measurement follows every chosen pulse.

### 2.3 Binary measurement outcomes

The paper uses

$$
k\in\{0,1\},
$$

where $k=0$ denotes the motional ground-state manifold and $k=1$ denotes the excited motional manifold. The main text does not distinguish $n\ge 1$ outcomes. If the dynamics simulation includes several motional Fock states, use

$$
\mathcal N_0=\{0\},\qquad
\mathcal N_1=\{1,2,\ldots,n_{\max}\}.
$$

### 2.4 Unnormalized branch matrices

For each pulse $a$ and measurement outcome $k$, define a nonnegative matrix

$$
B_{a,k}\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{N_S\times N_S}
$$

with entries

$$
(B_{a,k})_{ji}
=
\sum_{n\in\mathcal N_k}
|\langle j,n|U_a|i,0\rangle|^2.
$$

Here $U_a$ is the joint molecule-motion propagator generated by pulse $a$. For a population vector $s$, define

$$
\widetilde s_{a,k}=B_{a,k}s,
$$

$$
p_k(s,a)=\mathbf 1^T\widetilde s_{a,k},
$$

and, when $p_k>0$,

$$
s_{a,k}^{\mathrm{cond}}
=
\frac{\widetilde s_{a,k}}{p_k}.
$$

This normalization follows Eq. (3) of the paper and Supplemental Eq. (S3). The main-text shorthand $S_{t+1}=\mathcal A_k^{(a)}S_t$ suppresses this normalization or uses an already normalized conditional map.

### 2.5 Optional blackbody-radiation propagation

Let $G_{\mathrm{BBR}}$ be the population-rate generator. With the convention that population vectors are columns,

$$
(G_{\mathrm{BBR}})_{ji}=R_{i\to j}\quad (j\ne i),
$$

$$
(G_{\mathrm{BBR}})_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}R_{i\to j}.
$$

The paper uses a repeated first-order propagator,

$$
T_{\mathrm{BBR}}(\Delta t)
\approx
\left(I+G_{\mathrm{BBR}}\,\delta t\right)^{\Delta t/\delta t}.
$$

For an action-dependent duration $\tau_a$, the implementation below uses

$$
s_{a,k}'=T_{\mathrm{BBR}}(\tau_a)s_{a,k}^{\mathrm{cond}}.
$$

This ordering is a best assumption; the supplement says pulse and BBR effects are propagated sequentially but does not state the exact split ordering in algorithmic form.

### 2.6 Termination condition

Let $\eta\in(0,1)$ be the state-preparation infidelity threshold. Terminate when

$$
\max_i s_i \ge 1-\eta.
$$

The paper sometimes calls $\eta$ the "purity threshold," although the implemented condition is a maximum-population/confidence threshold, not $\operatorname{tr}(\rho^2)$.

### 2.7 Reward

The base reward is

$$
r=-1
$$

per pulse-measurement step, including the terminal step. This minimizes expected episode length.

For larger systems, the supplement adds a physics-informed penalty when the old and new population vectors have large cosine overlap,

$$
o(s,s')=\frac{s^Ts'}{\|s\|_2\|s'\|_2},
$$

with the stated trigger

$$
o(s,s')>1-\frac{1}{N_S}.
$$

The supplement does not provide an explicit final algebraic reward formula. A direct implementation assumption is

$$
r(s,a,k)
=
-1-r_o\,\mathbf 1\left[o(s,s_{a,k}')>1-\frac1{N_S}\right],
$$

where $r_o\ge 0$ is tuned. Keep this reward hook configurable.

## 3. Physics preprocessing: build branch matrices

The paper computes the population transition matrices before RL training by solving the time-dependent Schrodinger equation for every pulse and every molecular basis state. Coherence is retained during each pulse simulation and discarded only after extracting post-pulse populations.

```text
PROCEDURE BUILD_BRANCH_MATRICES(
    molecular_basis {|i>}_{i=1}^{N_S},
    motional_basis {|n>}_{n=0}^{n_max},
    pulse_library {pulse[a]}_{a=0}^{N_A-1},
    outcome_sets N_0={0}, N_1={1,...,n_max}
):

    INITIALIZE B[a][k] as zero matrices of shape (N_S, N_S)

    FOR each action a in {0,...,N_A-1}:
        CONSTRUCT the time-dependent total Hamiltonian
            H_a(t) = H_mol tensor I_mot
                   + I_mol tensor H_mot
                   + H_int^(a)(t)

        LET tau_pulse = pulse[a].duration

        FOR each initial molecular index i in {1,...,N_S}:
            psi_0 = |i> tensor |0>

            # Paper: solve the coherent time-dependent dynamics with QuTiP.
            # Suggested equivalent operations: sesolve or propagator.
            psi_f = SOLVE_SCHRODINGER(H_a(t), psi_0, t in [0,tau_pulse])

            FOR each final molecular index j in {1,...,N_S}:
                FOR k in {0,1}:
                    probability_mass = 0
                    FOR each motional number n in N_k:
                        amplitude = <j,n | psi_f>
                        probability_mass += |amplitude|^2
                    END FOR

                    # Column i is the output population generated by input |i>.
                    B[a][k][j,i] = probability_mass
                END FOR
            END FOR
        END FOR

        # Numerical physicality checks for every input basis state.
        FOR i in {1,...,N_S}:
            total_mass = SUM_k SUM_j B[a][k][j,i]
            ASSERT |total_mass - 1| <= propagation_tolerance
            ASSERT every B[a][k][j,i] >= -small_numerical_tolerance
        END FOR

        CLIP tiny negative roundoff values to zero
    END FOR

    RETURN B
END PROCEDURE
```

### QuTiP settings reported in the supplement

The supplement reports:

- QuTiP 4.7;
- `qutip.sesolve` and `qutip.propagator`;
- SciPy `zvode` integrator;
- time step approximately $1\,\mu\mathrm{s}$;
- within-manifold tolerances approximately `atol=1e-8`, `rtol=1e-6`;
- cross-manifold tolerances approximately `atol=1e-6`, `rtol=1e-4`;
- coherences retained during pulse propagation;
- diagonal populations extracted afterward;
- several motional states may be retained for numerical stability, even though the measurement is aggregated into $k=0$ versus $k\ge 1$.

## 4. Optional BBR matrix construction

```text
PROCEDURE BUILD_BBR_PROPAGATOR(transition_rates R, duration Delta_t, microstep delta_t):
    # R[i,j] means R_{i -> j}; set R[i,i]=0.
    INITIALIZE G as zeros(N_S,N_S)

    FOR i in {1,...,N_S}:
        FOR j in {1,...,N_S}, j != i:
            G[j,i] = R[i,j]
        END FOR
        G[i,i] = -SUM_{j != i} R[i,j]
    END FOR

    M = ROUND(Delta_t / delta_t)
    T = MATRIX_POWER(I + G*delta_t, M)

    # Numerical safeguards.
    CLIP tiny negative entries to zero
    RENORMALIZE each column of T to sum to one

    RETURN T
END PROCEDURE
```

For action-dependent pulse durations, precompute `T_BBR[a]` for every action. If the experiment includes a significant fixed readout/cooling time, replace `duration = pulse_duration[a]` by the full step duration. The paper does not specify this bookkeeping detail.

## 5. Branch computation used by both Gym and qMDP

```text
RECORD Branch:
    outcome_k          # 0 or 1
    probability        # p_k(s,a)
    next_state         # normalized population vector
    reward
    terminated
    terminal_index     # argmax_i next_state[i], if terminated

PROCEDURE COMPUTE_BRANCHES(state s, action a, model B, config):
    ASSERT s_i >= 0 and |SUM_i s_i - 1| <= tolerance
    branches = empty list

    FOR k in {0,1}:
        raw = B[a][k] @ s
        p_k = SUM_j raw[j]

        IF p_k <= config.zero_probability_tolerance:
            CONTINUE
        END IF

        s_cond = raw / p_k

        IF config.use_BBR:
            s_next = T_BBR[a] @ s_cond
            CLIP tiny negative entries to zero
            s_next = s_next / SUM_j s_next[j]
        ELSE:
            s_next = s_cond
        END IF

        reward = -1.0

        IF config.use_overlap_penalty:
            overlap = DOT(s,s_next) / (NORM2(s)*NORM2(s_next))
            IF overlap > 1 - 1/N_S:
                reward = reward - config.r_overlap
            END IF
        END IF

        max_population = MAX_i s_next[i]
        terminated = (max_population >= 1 - config.eta)

        IF terminated:
            terminal_index = ARGMAX_i s_next[i]
        ELSE:
            terminal_index = NONE
        END IF

        APPEND Branch(k, p_k, s_next, reward, terminated, terminal_index)
    END FOR

    total_probability = SUM_{branch in branches} branch.probability
    ASSERT |total_probability - 1| <= probability_tolerance

    # Absorb small numerical normalization errors into p_k.
    FOR branch in branches:
        branch.probability /= total_probability
    END FOR

    RETURN branches
END PROCEDURE
```

## 6. Gymnasium environment pseudocode

```text
CLASS RLQLSEnv EXTENDS gymnasium.Env:

    METHOD __init__(energies, B, pulse_durations, config, optional_BBR_rates):
        self.N_S = LENGTH(energies)
        self.N_A = NUMBER_OF_ACTIONS(B)
        self.energies = energies
        self.B = B
        self.config = config

        self.observation_space = Box(
            low=0.0,
            high=1.0,
            shape=(self.N_S,),
            dtype=float64
        )
        self.action_space = Discrete(self.N_A)

        self.s_initial = BOLTZMANN_POPULATION(energies, config.T_initial)

        IF config.use_BBR:
            FOR action a:
                self.T_BBR[a] = BUILD_BBR_PROPAGATOR(
                    optional_BBR_rates,
                    duration=pulse_durations[a],
                    microstep=config.BBR_delta_t
                )
            END FOR

        self.s = COPY(self.s_initial)
        self.step_count = 0

    METHOD reset(seed=None, options=None):
        CALL super().reset(seed=seed)
        self.s = COPY(self.s_initial)
        self.step_count = 0

        info = {
            "purity_proxy": MAX_i self.s[i],
            "terminal_index": NONE
        }
        RETURN COPY(self.s), info

    METHOD branch_model(state, action):
        # Deterministic model query required by the paper's qMDP backup.
        RETURN COMPUTE_BRANCHES(state, action, self.B, self.config)

    METHOD step(action):
        ASSERT self.action_space.contains(action)

        old_state = COPY(self.s)
        branches = self.branch_model(old_state, action)

        probabilities = [branch.probability for branch in branches]
        sampled_branch_index = self.np_random.choice(
            LENGTH(branches), probabilities=probabilities
        )
        realized = branches[sampled_branch_index]

        self.s = COPY(realized.next_state)
        self.step_count += 1

        # Prefer an external Gymnasium TimeLimit wrapper. If an internal
        # finite horizon is used, it is a truncation, not physical termination.
        truncated = (
            self.config.max_steps is not NONE
            AND self.step_count >= self.config.max_steps
            AND NOT realized.terminated
        )

        info = {
            "measurement_outcome": realized.outcome_k,
            "measurement_probabilities": {
                branch.outcome_k: branch.probability for branch in branches
            },
            "all_branches": DEEP_COPY(branches),
            "purity_proxy": MAX_i self.s[i],
            "terminal_index": realized.terminal_index,
            "step_count": self.step_count
        }

        RETURN (
            COPY(self.s),
            realized.reward,
            realized.terminated,
            truncated,
            info
        )
END CLASS
```

### Why `all_branches` is returned in `info`

Ordinary Gym interaction reveals only the realized branch. The paper's qMDP temporal-difference target explicitly uses both $k=0$ and $k=1$ branches and their probabilities. The trainer therefore needs either:

1. `env.unwrapped.branch_model(s,a)`, or
2. `info["all_branches"]` computed before the branch is sampled.

The sampled branch still determines the actual trajectory and hence the next action-selection state.

## 7. Q-network and target network

The paper reports a fully connected network with three layers and 128 nodes per layer, and tests deeper/wider alternatives. A concrete assumption is three hidden layers:

```text
CLASS QNetwork:
    layer_1 = Linear(N_S, 128)
    layer_2 = Linear(128, 128)
    layer_3 = Linear(128, 128)
    output  = Linear(128, N_A)

    METHOD forward(s):
        x = ReLU(layer_1(s))
        x = ReLU(layer_2(x))
        x = ReLU(layer_3(x))
        RETURN output(x)     # one Q-value per pulse action
END CLASS
```

The activation function is not specified in the paper; ReLU is a standard implementation assumption. The phrase "three-layer" is also not explicit about whether the output layer is counted. Keep the architecture configurable.

Initialize

```text
online_Q = QNetwork()
target_Q = DEEP_COPY(online_Q)
FREEZE_GRADIENTS(target_Q)
```

## 9. Epsilon-greedy action selection

The supplement prints an exponential schedule whose sign would make the initial epsilon incorrect. Use the monotone form

$$
\epsilon(n)
=
\epsilon_{\mathrm{end}}
+
(\epsilon_{\mathrm{start}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{end}})
\exp(-n/\tau_\epsilon),
$$

with

$$
\epsilon_{\mathrm{start}}=1,
\qquad
\tau_\epsilon=0.3N_{\mathrm{training}}.
$$

```text
PROCEDURE EPSILON_BY_EPISODE(episode_index, config):
    RETURN config.epsilon_end
         + (config.epsilon_start - config.epsilon_end)
           * EXP(-episode_index / config.epsilon_decay_constant)
END PROCEDURE

PROCEDURE SELECT_ACTION(state, online_Q, epsilon, rng, N_A):
    IF rng.uniform(0,1) < epsilon:
        RETURN rng.randint(0, N_A-1)
    ELSE:
        WITH no_gradient:
            RETURN ARGMAX_a online_Q(state)[a]
    END IF
END PROCEDURE
```

## 10. Ordinary sampled Double-DQN update

This is the implementation counterpart of Supplemental Eq. (S17), enhanced by the supplement's stated use of double-Q networks.

```text
PROCEDURE OPTIMIZE_SAMPLED_DDQN(
    replay_buffer,
    online_Q,
    target_Q,
    optimizer,
    batch_size,
    gamma,
    loss_type
):
    IF replay_buffer.size < batch_size:
        RETURN
    END IF

    batch = replay_buffer.sample(batch_size)

    q_pred = online_Q(batch.state).gather(batch.action)

    WITH no_gradient:
        next_action = ARGMAX_over_actions online_Q(batch.next_state)
        next_value = target_Q(batch.next_state).gather(next_action)
        target = batch.reward
               + gamma * (1 - batch.terminated) * next_value
    END WITH

    loss = HUBER_OR_MSE(q_pred, target, loss_type)

    optimizer.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()

    RETURN loss
END PROCEDURE
```

## 11. Expected-branch qMDP Double-DQN update

For the basic reward $r=-1$, Supplemental Eq. (S18) is

$$
Q(s,a)\leftarrow
-1
+
\gamma\sum_k p_k(s,a)
\max_{a'}Q(s_k',a').
$$

To combine this with double DQN, use the online network for action selection and the target network for evaluation. To support the branch-dependent overlap reward and branch-dependent termination, use

$$
y(s,a)
=
\sum_k p_k(s,a)
\left[
 r_k
 +
 \gamma(1-d_k)
 Q_{\bar\theta}
 \left(s_k',
 \arg\max_{a'}Q_\theta(s_k',a')
 \right)
\right],
$$

where $d_k\in\{0,1\}$ is the terminal flag for branch $k$.

```text
PROCEDURE OPTIMIZE_QMDP_DDQN(
    branch_replay_buffer,
    online_Q,
    target_Q,
    optimizer,
    batch_size,
    gamma,
    loss_type
):
    IF branch_replay_buffer.size < batch_size:
        RETURN
    END IF

    batch = branch_replay_buffer.sample(batch_size)

    predicted_values = empty vector length batch_size
    target_values = empty vector length batch_size

    FOR b in {1,...,batch_size}:
        s = batch[b].state
        a = batch[b].action

        predicted_values[b] = online_Q(s)[a]

        expected_target = 0

        WITH no_gradient:
            FOR branch in batch[b].branches:
                s_k = branch.next_state
                p_k = branch.probability
                r_k = branch.reward
                d_k = branch.terminated

                IF d_k:
                    continuation = 0
                ELSE:
                    best_next_action = ARGMAX_a2 online_Q(s_k)[a2]
                    continuation = target_Q(s_k)[best_next_action]
                END IF

                expected_target += p_k * (r_k + gamma*continuation)
            END FOR
        END WITH

        target_values[b] = expected_target
    END FOR

    loss = HUBER_OR_MSE(predicted_values, target_values, loss_type)

    optimizer.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()

    RETURN loss
END PROCEDURE
```

### Important implementation point

Do not store only the branch realized by `env.step()` if reproducing the qMDP method. The qMDP target needs the complete branch list. The realized branch is used only to advance the interactive trajectory.

## 12. Soft target-network update

The supplement states that a neural-network update rate $\tau$ is tuned and that double-Q networks are used. A standard soft update is

$$
\bar\theta\leftarrow
\tau_{\mathrm{target}}\theta
+(1-\tau_{\mathrm{target}})\bar\theta.
$$

```text
PROCEDURE SOFT_UPDATE_TARGET(online_Q, target_Q, tau_target):
    FOR each parameter pair (theta, theta_target):
        theta_target = tau_target*theta + (1-tau_target)*theta_target
    END FOR
END PROCEDURE
```

## 13. Full qMDP training loop

```text
PROCEDURE TRAIN_RL_QLS_QMDP(env, config):
    SET all random seeds

    online_Q = QNetwork(config.N_S, config.N_A)
    target_Q = DEEP_COPY(online_Q)

    # Optimizer type is not stated in the paper; Adam is a reasonable default.
    optimizer = Adam(online_Q.parameters, learning_rate=config.learning_rate)

    replay = ReplayBuffer(capacity=config.replay_capacity)

    training_log = empty list

    FOR episode in {0,...,config.num_training_episodes-1}:
        state, reset_info = env.reset()
        epsilon = EPSILON_BY_EPISODE(episode, config)
        episode_return = 0
        episode_length = 0
        terminated = FALSE
        truncated = FALSE

        WHILE NOT terminated AND NOT truncated:
            action = SELECT_ACTION(
                state,
                online_Q,
                epsilon,
                env.np_random,
                config.N_A
            )

            next_state, sampled_reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)

            # Store the full model branches, not only the sampled branch.
            replay.push(
                BranchTransition(
                    state=COPY(state),
                    action=action,
                    branches=DEEP_COPY(info["all_branches"])
                )
            )

            loss = OPTIMIZE_QMDP_DDQN(
                replay,
                online_Q,
                target_Q,
                optimizer,
                config.batch_size,
                config.gamma,
                config.loss_type
            )

            IF loss was computed:
                SOFT_UPDATE_TARGET(online_Q, target_Q, config.tau_target)
            END IF

            # The actual episode follows the sampled projective outcome.
            state = next_state
            episode_return += sampled_reward
            episode_length += 1
        END WHILE

        APPEND to training_log:
            episode,
            epsilon,
            episode_length,
            episode_return,
            terminated,
            truncated,
            final_purity_proxy=MAX_i state[i],
            final_state_index=ARGMAX_i state[i]

        IF episode is an evaluation checkpoint:
            metrics = EVALUATE_GREEDY_POLICY(env, online_Q, config)
            SAVE checkpoint and metrics
        END IF
    END FOR

    RETURN online_Q, training_log
END PROCEDURE
```

## 14. Full ordinary sampled-DQN training loop

The same interactive environment can reproduce the paper's ordinary MDP modeling by replacing the replay item and optimizer call.

```text
PROCEDURE TRAIN_RL_QLS_SAMPLED_MDP(env, config):
    INITIALIZE online_Q, target_Q, optimizer, replay

    FOR each training episode:
        state, info = env.reset()
        epsilon = EPSILON_BY_EPISODE(episode, config)

        WHILE episode is active:
            action = SELECT_ACTION(state, online_Q, epsilon, rng, N_A)
            next_state, reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)

            replay.push(
                SampledTransition(
                    state,
                    action,
                    reward,
                    next_state,
                    terminated
                )
            )

            OPTIMIZE_SAMPLED_DDQN(...)
            SOFT_UPDATE_TARGET(...)

            state = next_state
        END WHILE
    END FOR
END PROCEDURE
```

The paper argues that sampled MDP and qMDP backups agree in the infinite-sample limit, while qMDP is more sample efficient because rare measurement branches are represented explicitly in every backup.

## 15. Greedy testing/evaluation

During testing the paper disables exploration and chooses actions deterministically from the learned Q-function.

```text
PROCEDURE EVALUATE_GREEDY_POLICY(env, Q, config):
    episode_lengths = empty list
    success_flags = empty list
    terminal_state_histogram = zeros(N_S)
    action_histogram = zeros(N_A)

    FOR test_episode in {1,...,config.num_test_episodes}:
        state, info = env.reset()
        terminated = FALSE
        truncated = FALSE
        length = 0

        WHILE NOT terminated AND NOT truncated:
            WITH no_gradient:
                action = ARGMAX_a Q(state)[a]
            END WITH

            action_histogram[action] += 1
            state, reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)
            length += 1
        END WHILE

        APPEND length to episode_lengths
        APPEND terminated to success_flags

        IF terminated:
            terminal_state_histogram[ARGMAX_i state[i]] += 1
        END IF
    END FOR

    RETURN {
        "mean_length": MEAN(episode_lengths),
        "median_length": MEDIAN(episode_lengths),
        "success_rate": MEAN(success_flags),
        "length_distribution": episode_lengths,
        "terminal_state_histogram": terminal_state_histogram,
        "action_histogram": action_histogram
    }
END PROCEDURE
```

The paper evaluates selected models with 1000 testing episodes in the small CaH+ example.

## 16. Sweeping baseline for verification

```text
PROCEDURE EVALUATE_SWEEPING_POLICY(env, ordered_pulse_cycle, num_episodes):
    FOR each episode:
        state, info = env.reset()
        pulse_pointer = 0

        WHILE episode is active:
            action = ordered_pulse_cycle[pulse_pointer]
            pulse_pointer = (pulse_pointer + 1) mod LENGTH(ordered_pulse_cycle)

            state, reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)
        END WHILE

        RECORD episode length and success
    END FOR

    RETURN aggregate metrics
END PROCEDURE
```

To match the paper's numerical comparison, perform a projective measurement after every pulse and use the full action/outcome history, represented by the current population vector, to check termination. This differs from the original NIST experimental sweeping protocol, which can place several pulses between measurements.

## 17. Hyperparameters explicitly reported by the paper

### Common structure

- Discrete DQN action space.
- Fully connected Q-network.
- Three layers with 128 nodes per layer, subject to the architecture ambiguity noted above.
- Experience replay.
- Double-Q networks.
- MSE or smooth-L1 loss.

## 19. Verification and unit-test checklist

### 19.1 Physics-map tests

```text
FOR every action a and input state index i:
    ASSERT B[a][k][j,i] >= 0 for all j,k
    ASSERT SUM_k SUM_j B[a][k][j,i] approximately equals 1
END FOR
```

For random simplex states $s$:

```text
branches = COMPUTE_BRANCHES(s,a)
ASSERT SUM_k branches[k].probability approximately equals 1
ASSERT every branch.next_state sums to 1
ASSERT every branch.next_state entry is nonnegative
```

### 19.2 Monte Carlo measurement test

For fixed $(s,a)$, sample the environment outcome many times without advancing the state and compare empirical frequencies to the analytic $p_k(s,a)$.

### 19.3 Terminal test

Construct $s$ with $\max_i s_i=1-\eta$ and confirm that the terminal inequality convention is exactly the desired one. The paper's wording uses probability greater than $1-\eta$ in one place and figures use a threshold convention; choose `>=` consistently.

### 19.4 BBR tests

- Each BBR propagator must preserve total population.
- Entries should be nonnegative after numerical correction.
- At long times, the propagator should move populations toward the appropriate thermal stationary distribution.

### 19.5 qMDP versus sampled-MDP consistency

On a small toy system, train both methods with very many sampled transitions. Their converged value functions/policies should approach each other, consistent with Supplemental Sec. SD.

### 19.6 Paper-level regression targets

Exact reproduction requires the authors' pulse matrices and all training settings, but approximate regression targets include:

- small CaH+ example: reported mean RL episode length approximately 8.3 versus sweeping approximately 9.7;
- H3O+ example: 130 states, 218 pulse actions, and about 80 percent termination within 62 pulses at the reported threshold;
- qMDP should train substantially more stably than sampled MDP for the H3O+ model.
